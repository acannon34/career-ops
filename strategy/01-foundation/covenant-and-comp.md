# 01 — Foundation

This gates both tracks. Do it **week one**. A restrictive covenant can take entire categories of PE portfolio company off the table and determines which of your own relationships you can legally pursue if you go independent. You want to know the rules before you spend months courting people you can't legally land.

---

## A. Covenant review

Pull your actual signed agreements (offer letter, MD/partner agreement, equity grant docs, any separate restrictive covenant agreement). Read the real language, not your memory of it.

Capture for each:

### Non-compete
- [ ] Does one exist? Scope (services? named competitors? geography?)
- [ ] Duration after departure: ____ months
- [ ] Does it reach PE *portfolio companies*, or only direct consulting competitors?
- [ ] Enforceability in your state (varies a lot — note your state: ____)

### Client non-solicit
- [ ] Duration: ____ months
- [ ] Scope: clients you personally served? Any firm client? Defined how?
- [ ] **This is the one that bites the independent path.** Can you approach your own relationships, and when?

### Employee non-solicit
- [ ] Duration: ____ months
- [ ] Can you recruit people you worked with (matters if you build a firm)?

### Notice & equity
- [ ] Required notice period: ____
- [ ] Garden leave provisions?
- [ ] Unvested equity forfeited on departure: $____
- [ ] Any clawback or vesting-acceleration triggers?

> Consider a one-hour consult with an employment lawyer in your state once you've read the docs. At MD level with a book of business, this is cheap insurance and the read on enforceability is genuinely state-specific.

## C. Separate your professional identity (in parallel)

Quietly, before anyone's looking:

- [ ] Personal email set up and in use for anything career-related
- [ ] Genuine professional network exported (your relationships, not firm CRM data)
- [ ] Personal copies of writing/IP that's yours to keep
- [ ] LinkedIn reflects you, not just the Accenture role (light touch — don't signal a search yet)
